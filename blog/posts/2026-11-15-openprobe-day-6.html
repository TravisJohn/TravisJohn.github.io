Build in Public, Day 6: Connecting the Pieces

Over the last five builds, I explored separate parts of the recruitment experience: preparing a hiring brief, capturing applicants’ work stories, an applicant home, a quiz room and reviewing evidence.

Today they came together in one local prototype.

One journey, end to end

Hiring brief → Application → Quiz → Recruiter review → Applicant outcome

Each stage now reads and writes the same saved data, so it can use what happened before it. Here’s what that looks like in practice:

  1. Hiring brief. The recruiter starts a new hiring project. The brief is interpreted by fixed rules, and if two sources disagree, for example on location, the recruiter has to settle the conflict and approve before anything opens.
  2. Application. Applicants see only a public invitation, never the private brief or requirements. They write their work stories, add a self-rated expertise level and salary expectation, then submit.
  3. Quiz. Five data engineering rounds with a 12-second vote window, scored on the server. The blue-ringed YOU dot sits among 999 simulated participants, and the crowd’s movement never touches the applicant’s actual score.
  4. Recruiter review. The recruiter sees the saved stories and quiz result under an alias, scores four rubric categories, and can shortlist or ask a clarifying question. A clarification reply sends the same application back to review.
  5. Applicant outcome. The recruiter previews an invitation or a decline reason, then explicitly confirms it. The applicant sees the outcome in their home feed. Only an invitation reveals the candidate’s name to the recruiter.

Reload the page or restart the server, and the application, answers, review and decision are all still there.

How it’s checked

  • Automated tests cover the full application-to-decision path, the conflict approval gate, who can see what, hidden answer keys, server-side scoring, recovery after a restart, deadline expiry and the clarification loop.
  • A zero quiz score doesn’t automatically reject anyone. A person still decides.
  • Internal reviewer notes never reach the applicant.

The automated workflow checks are passing. A full browser walkthrough, on desktop and mobile, is the next validation step.

My biggest lesson: small experiments beat big designs

Isolated proofs of concept help me work through complicated projects.

When I try to solve the whole project in my head, overthinking leaves me stuck. Breaking it into smaller experiments gives me something concrete to build, test and learn from.

Each prototype answers a smaller question. The brief studio asked how a recruiter handles a messy brief, and the quiz room asked whether screening could feel interactive. Connecting them then shows what the complete system actually needs, like shared records, ownership rules and a clear hand-off between stages.

Another lesson: “agentic” still needs ordinary code

Much of an “agentic” solution is ordinary, deterministic code. Timing, scoring, saving progress and moving between stages all need clear rules.

I’ve kept a list of where agents could help later, along with what stands in for them today:

Where an agent could helpWhat the prototype does today
Investigating ambiguous briefsA rule-based interpreter extracts known fields, flags conflicts and leaves the rest for the recruiter
Spotting gaps in candidate evidenceAnswers sit under fixed rubric categories, and the recruiter judges whether they’re enough
Choosing adaptive follow-up questionsThe recruiter picks a prepared question or writes an approved one
Drafting narrative assessmentsOriginal answer excerpts are shown directly, with no invented AI verdict or confidence score
Wording invitations and outcomesAn approved template plus feedback the reviewer writes

For now, fixed rules and human review keep the prototype usable. When AI comes in, versioning and reviewer approval stay.

Status

  • This is a local prototype with fictional records and demo identities.
  • There’s no live AI, voice, real multiplayer, payments, ATS integration or outbound messages.
  • Quiz scores are demonstrations, not validated hiring predictors.
  • Next up is the browser walkthrough, then testing a bounded brief analyst against today’s rules.

Wrapping up Day 6

I’m finishing Day 6 with a connected prototype and a development habit I want to keep: make the uncertainty small enough to test.
