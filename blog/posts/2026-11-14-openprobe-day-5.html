Build in Public, Day 5: A Screening Room With 1,000 People in It

On Day 4 I built the applicant’s home feed. Today’s OpenProbe build is something more playful: a simulated screening room for data engineering.

The problem

Most skills screening is a quiet form or a timed quiz. It tells you little about how someone thinks, and it’s a dull way for an applicant to show what they know. I wanted to explore whether screening could feel more interactive while giving applicants another way to demonstrate practical knowledge.

What’s in the room

The latest proof of concept brings together:

  • Five data engineering rounds, each with circular answer zones.
  • Randomised reveals, so the question and its answers appear in a different order.
  • Scoring that rewards correctness first, with a bonus for speed.
  • A room of 1,000 participants, with one real user and 999 simulated participants.
  • Tiny coloured dots with organic movement, plus a distinctive “YOU” marker so applicants can always find themselves.
  • A full-screen arena to help participants focus.

The crowd experiment

On selected rounds, simulated distractors drift towards plausible but incorrect answers. I want to explore how seeing those choices affects the experience and an applicant’s decisions.

It’s a question every quiz show has played with. When most of the room heads one way, do you trust your own answer or follow the crowd? In a hiring context, someone who sticks to a well-reasoned answer under social pressure is showing something a CV can’t. This is also why OpenProbe discloses this kind of influence test up front. It’s part of the design, not a trick.

Status

  • This is a playable, text-based proof of concept.
  • Voice is parked for now.
  • Real multiplayer at scale is still to be built and tested.
  • The 999 other participants are simulated.

Where this fits

The intention is to build another source of evidence that sits alongside the applicant’s background and accomplishments, not to replace them. First I’m testing pacing, clarity and the overall experience. Then I’ll connect the arena with what’s already prototyped: the recruiter setup, the applicant journey and the review screens.

Another piece of OpenProbe is taking shape.

Get involved

OpenProbe is open source under the MIT licence. Explore the repo on GitHub, open an issue or share your take.
