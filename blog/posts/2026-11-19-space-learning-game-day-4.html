Build in Public, Day 4: From a Spaceship You Can Walk Around to a Dogfight You Can Fly

I’m building DRIFT, an open-source space game in Three.js and TypeScript. You play a maintenance drone aboard a research freighter. The crew is asleep, the engines are humming, and you have the run of the ship. Head to the bridge, though, and the holographic command deck can put you in a dogfight.

On Day 3 the ship and the drone got a makeover. Yesterday I worked on how the ship flies. Today I worked on how it feels to be alone aboard it.

Yesterday: the ship, and a fight you can fly

Yesterday started with thrusters and a beacon for the drone, plating for the freighter, and a synthesized engine hum. I added a soft tether that glows red and beeps faster as you drift too far from the hull.

By the afternoon, you could set a course from the command deck, switch to the exterior camera, or open Tactical and fly a combat drill. Press C and the same fight switches from a top-down hologram to a 120° first-person cockpit.

The flight model changed twice

Pure momentum sounded right on paper. In my hands, I spent more time correcting drift than lining up a shot. Speed notches made the ship easier to fly.

Then two playtest notes shaped the fight:

  • “There are no enemies in front of me” gave the raiders stations ahead of the ship and timed attack runs.
  • “Let me dodge” became a sideways boost with just enough energy to make the timing matter.

Today: the cockpit pass

Today’s cockpit pass started with what I could see. I refined the cockpit framing, added a ringed planet and a distant nebula, and gave passing asteroids chipped edges and impact craters. The enemy fighters got swept wings, a visible canopy and an engine glow you can see as they fly away.

Between those details, the red INCOMING brackets and the map on the centre console, I could follow a raider through its pass.

Screens you can read from the doorway

Back on the bridge, three blurry screens became instruments I could read from across the room:

  • POWER shows the load bars.
  • HELM plots a course.
  • COMMS carries a waveform.

My favourite moment: the power cooldown

It came in the last playtest. Every two minutes, a robotic female voice comes over the intercom:

“Power cooldown cycle in five, four, three, two, one.”

The cabin lights fade. For fourteen seconds, the stars outside the window, the floor guides and the drone’s headlamp stand out. Then the lights slowly return.

You can trigger it yourself from the Ship tab, turn off the automatic cycle, or mute the voice.

What I’m taking away

  • “Let me dodge” changed how a fight plays.
  • “Those screens look blurry” changed what I could read from the doorway.
  • One countdown gave an empty bridge its own rhythm.

Those are the kinds of details I want to keep finding through playtesting.

Status

The latest build passed my playtest, and 714 automated tests are green, up from 707 at the combat checkpoint.
